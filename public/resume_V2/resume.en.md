## Technical Skills & Working Methods

### How I Work with Agents

I often use voice input to explain the background, intended result and constraints, then work with an agent to turn that into actionable tasks. I check names, numbers, dates and important constraints in writing so transcription errors or misunderstandings do not carry into implementation.

For ongoing projects, I use Markdown, project rules and Git to preserve requirements, decisions and handoffs. I break goals into tasks that can be checked, define the change boundaries and completion criteria, and work with agents on implementation, testing, debugging and deployment.

### Adoption and Validation

I start by understanding the existing workflow and choosing a task whose results people can compare. Videos, presentations and front-end prototypes have been useful entry points in my educational setting. Once people can see a result, it becomes easier to discuss the process behind it.

I also ask how output will be checked, how failures will be handled and where human judgment is still needed. I consider generation, review and correction together when assessing efficiency. Human review can be a reasonable part of the design.

## Proposed Training: Increasing Your AI Leverage

This is a topic I want to develop into a course. It has not yet been delivered as a complete course.

I want colleagues to find a useful next step from their current level. Beginners can practice with one real task. More experienced users can compare models, divide work into smaller tasks and turn successful methods into repeatable workflows.

### Practice Explaining the Task

I frequently use voice input because it lets me express a connected train of thought. I would help colleagues practice explaining the context, goal, available information and constraints, then check what the AI understood. The important skill is communicating and confirming intent. The input method can depend on the person and the situation.

### Understand Where Tools Reach Their Limits

I would use concrete examples to show common failures and when it is worth changing tools, models or how a task is divided. Comparisons should include waiting time, cost and manual correction. I would adjust technical explanations to the audience and avoid presenting guesses about undisclosed model architectures as facts.

### Build Methods That Can Be Reused

At the beginning, clarify which information can be used, what must not be uploaded and which outputs need human review. Once a task works reliably, document the materials, steps and checks as an SOP. More advanced users can explore tool integration, project context and failure handling.

I follow changes in AI tools and models, compare them on real tasks and update my methods and teaching materials. I want colleagues to develop judgment they can apply to their own work.

## Draft Appendix: Editorial Feedback and Evidence to Add

The following feedback was prepared by an AI assistant from the material currently available. It is included for the author’s résumé revisions and interview preparation. It is not an independent assessment or a claim that the suggested work has been completed.

### The Strongest Combination So Far

You have AI adoption experience inside an organization, working products and a research foundation. LumaReader deserves an early place because an interviewer can experience its interface while reading. For interviews, choose one project and explain its requirements, your contribution, technical tradeoffs and acceptance results in detail.

### Make AI Leverage Concrete

“Increasing AI leverage” can work as a teaching topic, but the résumé needs a measurable example. Record how long a task took before, how long it takes with AI including review and correction, and whether the delivered quality changes. The current material does not support a reliable productivity multiplier.

### Show More Engineering Evidence

The products show that you can deliver working results. An engineering team will also need to understand the data flow, failure points, tests and recovery decisions. The document processing platform already includes controlled concurrency and recovery, making it a useful starting case.

### Separate Personal Use from Market Evidence

Frequent personal use and feedback from friends support the case that the product solves some real usability problems. Claims about adoption at scale still need download definitions, observation dates and evidence of continued use. “Downloads are doing well” is not enough to establish market success.

### Make Technical Explanations More Precise

Generative models use different architectures. Image and video generation should not all be described as one convolution or compression pipeline. Start with observable limitations, then explain the relevant mechanisms. The [Latent Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752) and [Diffusion Transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748) papers provide useful examples of architectural differences.

### Tailor the Evidence to the Role

AI application and adoption roles can emphasize delivery, use and workflow improvements. For ML engineering roles, add a recent, reproducible data and model evaluation project. Doctoral applications need a clearer research question, research outputs and individual contribution. This working draft can retain the full material, while later applications can be shortened for each audience.
